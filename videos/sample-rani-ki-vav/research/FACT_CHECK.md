# Fact check: Rani ki Vav sample (Gate 2, checked 22 Nov 2026)

Status: **PASS_WITH_QUALIFICATIONS**. Every claim has 2+ sources opened on 22 Nov 2026.

| # | Claim in script | Verdict | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Buried for more than 600 years | Supported (sources say "over six centuries" and "almost seven"; script uses the safer "600 se zyada") | Wikipedia; UNESCO/ICOMOS evaluation |
| 2 | In Patan, Gujarat | Supported | Wikipedia; Incredible India; Sahapedia |
| 3 | Built in the 11th century by Rani Udayamati in memory of her husband Raja Bhimdev I (Bhima I) | Supported. Qualifier: Wikipedia says construction is "attributed" to her; exact year differs by source (1032 vs 1063), so the script says only "11th century" | Sahapedia; Wikipedia; Incredible India; UNESCO/ICOMOS ("memorial, 11th century") |
| 4 | About 65 m long, 28 m deep | Supported. Qualifier: UNESCO gives 70 m including surroundings; script says "kareeb" | Wikipedia; Sahapedia |
| 5 | Seven levels | Supported. Qualifier: Sahapedia says only five survive; script says "saat manzil" which is the design | Wikipedia; UNESCO/ICOMOS; Sahapedia |
| 6 | About 400 large sculptures survive today | Supported | Sahapedia ("some 400"); UNESCO/ICOMOS ("approximately 400 survive") |
| 7 | Saraswati river floods buried it in silt | Supported | Wikipedia; Sahapedia; UNESCO/ICOMOS |
| 8 | Seen again in 1940s excavations under Baroda State | Supported | Wikipedia; Incredible India (1940s) |
| 9 | ASI removed the silt in the 1980s | Supported | Wikipedia (1981–87); Sahapedia; UNESCO/ICOMOS |
| 10 | UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2014 | Supported (22 June 2014) | Wikipedia; Sahapedia; Incredible India |
| 11 | July 2018: RBI issued a new lavender ₹100 note with Rani ki Vav on the back | Supported. RBI announced on 19 July 2018 that it would issue the note shortly | RBI press release 19 Jul 2018; Wikipedia |
| 12 | "shayad abhi aapki jeb mein hai" | Not a factual claim, hedged with "shayad" (old ₹100 notes are also still legal tender) | — |
